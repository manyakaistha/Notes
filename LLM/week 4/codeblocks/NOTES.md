# Week 4 counting and source notes

- Devlin et al. (2019) describe a 30,000-token WordPiece vocabulary and report rounded totals of 110M (base) and 340M (large). The ebook's architecture table gives 30,522, the standard released BERT vocabulary size. Both are counted here: the model-formula counts at 30,000 are 109,081,344 (base) and 334,607,360 (large); with 30,522 entries, both formula and PyTorch module give 109,482,240 and 335,141,888.
- The exact 30,522-vocabulary counts round to the paper headlines 110M and 340M. The configured counts include token, 512-position and two-segment embeddings, the embedding LayerNorm, all encoder Q/K/V/output and FFN linear biases, two affine LayerNorms per block, and the pooler. They exclude the separate pretraining MLM transform/decoder and NSP classifier heads, which are not part of the base encoder-plus-pooler parameter convention.
- The ebook's practice calculation uses 30,000 but estimates 108M and attributes the gap to LayerNorm and the MLM head. The complete count specified above is 109,081,344 at 30,000; the difference from 110M is consistent with rounding and head-counting conventions, while the additional 522 configured vocabulary rows add 400,896 in base and 534,528 in large (522*768 and 522*1024 respectively).
- Sampling convention: top-p keeps the smallest descending-probability prefix whose cumulative mass is at least the threshold, including the first crossing token; ties are resolved by NumPy's stable sort order for this toy vector. The figure uses the stated convention.
