## 2. Week 1 Diagnostic Verification

Before introducing signal processing math, Week 1 fundamentals must be validated. If the biological premise is flawed, downstream processing becomes scientifically meaningless.

### Diagnostic Questions & Authoritative Solutions

#### 1. Fundamental Difference: Neural Activity vs. Neural Data
- **Neural Activity:** The underlying biological process occurring in the nervous system—membrane ion flux, action potentials, post-synaptic potentials, and coordinated population firing.
- **Neural Data:** The digitized measurement or physical representation captured by an instrument (EEG electrodes, fMRI scanner, ECoG grid).
- **Physical Chain:** Neurons generate electrical fields $\rightarrow$ electrodes detect potential differences $\rightarrow$ hardware digitizes the voltages into an array. Neural activity is the biological ground truth; neural data is an indirect, noisy measurement.

#### 2. Physical Meaning of a `[64, 5000]` EEG Matrix
- `64` represents the **spatial sensors** (number of physical recording channels/electrodes on the scalp montage).
- `5000` represents the **temporal samples** (consecutive discrete voltage measurements recorded over time on each channel).
- Geometry: $\text{[Channels} \times \text{Time]}$.

#### 3. Inter-Sample Interval at 250 Hz
- Sampling rate $f_s = 250\text{ Hz} = 250\text{ samples/second}$.
- The time interval between sample index $10$ and sample index $11$ is:
  $$\Delta t = \frac{1}{f_s} = \frac{1}{250} = 0.004\text{ s} = 4\text{ ms}$$

#### 4. The Amplitude Misconception ("Brain Working Harder")
- **The Dangerous Misconception:** "A massive surge in EEG amplitude proves the subject's brain is working harder."
- **Why this is false:** Scalp EEG measures aggregate synchronous dipoles, not raw computational effort. High amplitude often reflects synchronized resting populations (e.g., occipital Alpha during quiet wakefulness) or slow sleep stages (Delta). Conversely, active cognitive engagement typically desynchronizes firing, resulting in lower-amplitude, higher-frequency activity. Furthermore, high amplitude frequently stems from non-neural artifacts (eye blinks, head motion, electrode pops, impedance changes).

#### 5. Alpha Band Characteristics
- **Frequency Range:** $8\text{–}12\text{ Hz}$.
- **Behavioral State:** Relaxed, quiet wakefulness with eyes closed.
- **Spatial Topography:** Prominent over posterior/occipital electrode sites ($O_1, O_2, O_z$). Opening eyes or active visual processing induces "alpha blocking" (attenuation).

#### 6. Inability of fMRI to Capture Millisecond Spiking
- fMRI does not measure electrical potentials; it measures the **Blood-Oxygen-Level-Dependent (BOLD)** hemodynamic response.
- Vascular response latency operates on the scale of seconds ($4\text{–}6\text{ s}$ peak), whereas neuronal action potentials fire on the order of milliseconds ($1\text{ ms}$).

#### 7. Motor Imagery Formulation (AI Problem Formulation)
- **Biological Event:** Cortical sensorimotor activation during imagined movement (e.g., imagining moving the left hand without overt muscular execution).
- **AI Label:** The assigned class identifier (e.g., `LEFT_HAND` vs. `RIGHT_HAND` vs. `REST`), defined by experimental protocol.
- **Core Principle:** The brain generates voltages; the experimental design provides labels. The AI learns $X_{\text{EEG}} \rightarrow y_{\text{label}}$.

#### 8. Spatial Information in Scalp EEG
- Scalp EEG possesses no intrinsic spatial coordinate grid; spatial information is derived exclusively from the known positions of physical electrodes (e.g., 10–20 international system).
- Because skull and tissue act as spatial volume conductors, potentials blur and mix. Reconstructing source generators requires computational inverse modeling.

## 3. Topic 1: What is a Signal?

Mathematically, a **signal** is a function that conveys information about the state or behavior of a physical system. It maps an independent variable to a dependent variable:
- **Independent Variable:** The domain along which change occurs (in EEG: time $t$, in seconds).
- **Dependent Variable:** The physical quantity being measured (in EEG: electrical potential difference / voltage $x(t)$, in microvolts $\mu\text{V}$).

### Continuous vs. Discrete Signals
1. **Continuous Signal ($x(t)$):** The biological reality. Extracellular voltages exist continuously across uninterrupted physical time.
2. **Discrete Signal ($x[n]$):** The computational representation. Digital hardware samples the continuous waveform at uniform intervals $n \cdot \Delta t$, yielding a finite sequence of numbers.

### The Defining Property of a Time Series: Temporal Order
A time series is not merely a collection of numbers; it is an **ordered sequence**:
$$X = [x(t_0), x(t_1), x(t_2), \dots, x(t_{N-1})]$$

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Order Encodes the Physics:** If you sort an array of 5,000 EEG voltage values from lowest to highest, global summary statistics (mean, variance, min, max) remain identical. However, the signal is destroyed: frequency, phase, waveform morphology, and temporal latency are permanently erased.

---

## 4. Signal vs. Noise: The Conceptual Divide

A recorded voltage trace is a composite mixture of multiple physical sources:
- **Signal:** The specific information relevant to the scientific or clinical hypothesis under investigation.
- **Noise:** Unwanted random variation or measurement uncertainty (e.g., thermal fluctuations in electrode leads or amplifier circuitry).
- **Interference / Artifact:** Structured, non-neural contamination (e.g., 50/60 Hz power-line electromagnetic hum, cranial muscle activation, eye blinks).

> [!NOTE]
> **The Golden Rule of Signal Processing:** "Noise" is not an intrinsic physical label; it is defined relative to the experimental hypothesis. If studying slow gastric or autonomic rhythms, a 1 Hz oscillation is the *signal* and 10 Hz Alpha is *noise*. If detecting cognitive Alpha desynchronization, 10 Hz is the *signal* and the 1 Hz drift is *noise*.

## 5. Python Laboratory: Generating and Diagnosing Signals

### Code Implementation (`generate_signals.py`)

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# 1. Define discrete time axis
fs = 250  # Sampling rate in Hz
duration = 2.0  # Duration in seconds
t = np.linspace(0, duration, int(fs * duration), endpoint=False)  # 500 samples

# 2. Signal A: Pure 10 Hz Alpha oscillation (5 uV peak)
signal_a = 5 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 10 * t)

# 3. Signal B: Signal A + Random Measurement Noise
np.random.seed(42)
random_noise = np.random.normal(0, 2.5, len(t))
signal_b = signal_a + random_noise

# 4. Signal C: Multi-component Realistic Mixture
# Adding slow 1 Hz baseline drift (sweat/movement) and 50 Hz power-line interference
slow_drift = 8 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 1 * t)
line_noise = 3 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 50 * t)
signal_c = signal_a + slow_drift + line_noise + random_noise

# Quantitative diagnostics
print(f"Sampling rate: {fs} Hz | Sample interval (dt): {1000/fs:.1f} ms")
print(f"Array shape: {signal_c.shape}")
print(f"Variance - Signal A (Pure): {np.var(signal_a):.2f} uV^2")
print(f"Variance - Signal B (Noisy): {np.var(signal_b):.2f} uV^2")
print(f"Variance - Signal C (Contaminated): {np.var(signal_c):.2f} uV^2")
```

### Quantitative Output & Interpretation

```text
Sampling rate: 250 Hz | Sample interval (dt): 4.0 ms
Array shape: (500,)
Variance - Signal A (Pure): 12.50 uV^2
Variance - Signal B (Noisy): 18.79 uV^2
Variance - Signal C (Contaminated): 54.55 uV^2
```

- **Signal A (Pure Alpha):** Theoretical variance for a sine wave with amplitude $A=5\mu\text{V}$ is $A^2 / 2 = 25 / 2 = 12.50\mu\text{V}^2$. The waveform is deterministic and periodic with period $T = 1/f = 100\text{ ms}$.
- **Signal B (Noisy Alpha):** The added zero-mean Gaussian noise ($\sigma=2.5\mu\text{V}$, theoretical variance $6.25\mu\text{V}^2$) increases total variance to $18.79\mu\text{V}^2 \approx 12.50 + 6.25$. The underlying 10 Hz rhythm remains visible despite local fuzziness.
- **Signal C (Real-World Mixture):** Adding the 1 Hz drift ($A=8\mu\text{V}$, variance $32\mu\text{V}^2$) and 50 Hz mains hum ($A=3\mu\text{V}$, variance $4.5\mu\text{V}^2$) inflates total variance to $54.55\mu\text{V}^2$. The 10 Hz Alpha wave is visually obscured by the slow wave and high-frequency carrier.

## 6. Day 1 Knowledge Checkpoint & Worked Solutions

### Question 1: Hypothesis-Dependent Noise
> *Look at `Signal C` in the code above. If a neuroscientist is conducting an experiment to study the 1 Hz slow drift (e.g., investigating gastric-brain coupling), which components of `Signal C` become the "noise"?*

**Authoritative Solution:**
Under this scientific hypothesis, the target signal is the **1 Hz slow drift**. All remaining components constitute unwanted variation or interference:
$$\text{Signal C} = \underbrace{\text{1 Hz drift}}_{\textbf{Signal of Interest}} + \underbrace{\text{10 Hz Alpha}}_{\text{Physiological Noise}} + \underbrace{\text{50 Hz Mains}}_{\text{Environmental Interference}} + \underbrace{\text{Random Gaussian Noise}}_{\text{Measurement Noise}}$$
- 1 Hz drift $\rightarrow$ **Signal**
- 10 Hz Alpha $\rightarrow$ **Noise** (unwanted cortical oscillation)
- 50 Hz Line Hum $\rightarrow$ **Interference**
- Gaussian Noise $\rightarrow$ **Noise**

### Question 2: Sorting 5,000 Measurements
> *I give you a 1D array of 5,000 voltage measurements. You accidentally sort the array from lowest voltage to highest voltage. Why is this data no longer a "time series," and what physical information have you permanently destroyed?*

**Authoritative Solution:**
A time series requires an explicit bijective mapping between each measurement and its acquisition timestamp $t_n = n \cdot \Delta t$. Sorting reorganizes the elements such that $x_0 \le x_1 \le \dots \le x_{4999}$. 
- **What is preserved:** The sample probability distribution (empirical CDF), sample mean $\mu$, sample variance $\sigma^2$, minimum, and maximum.
- **What is permanently destroyed:**
  1. The chronological sequence of potential fluctuations.
  2. All oscillatory frequencies and phase angles.
  3. Signal derivative, velocity, and waveform morphology.
  4. Precise latencies of transient physiological events (spikes, ERPs).
The array is reduced to an unindexed histogram of voltage amplitudes; the time series ceases to exist.

### Question 3: Physical Meaning of $x(t) = 5\sin(2\pi 10 t)$
> *In the mathematical equation for `signal_a`, what exactly does the `10` represent physically, and what does the `5` represent physically?*

**Authoritative Solution:**
- **The value `10` represents the oscillation frequency ($f = 10\text{ Hz}$):** The physical system completes 10 complete sinusoidal cycles per second. Its fundamental period is $T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{10} = 0.1\text{ s} = 100\text{ ms}$.
- **The value `5` represents the peak amplitude ($A = 5\mu\text{V}$):** The maximum instantaneous potential difference relative to baseline. The signal oscillates within the range $[-5\mu\text{V}, +5\mu\text{V}]$, yielding a peak-to-peak amplitude $A_{p-p} = 10\mu\text{V}$.
