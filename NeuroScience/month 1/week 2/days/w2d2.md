## 2. Topic 1: The Basic Nyquist Intuition & Aliasing

### The Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Theorem
To uniquely reconstruct a continuous band-limited signal from discrete samples, the sampling rate ($f_s$) must be strictly greater than twice the highest frequency component ($f_{\max}$) present in the signal:

$$f_s > 2 f_{\max}$$

The theoretical boundary $2 f_{\max}$ is known as the **Nyquist rate**, and $f_{N} = f_s / 2$ is the **Nyquist frequency**.

### Why Aliasing Occurs
If a signal is sampled below the Nyquist rate ($f_s \le 2 f_{\max}$), fewer than two samples occur per cycle. When those discrete points are connected or processed by digital algorithms, high-frequency oscillations fold back into lower frequencies. The apparent frequency of the aliased component is given by:

$$f_{\text{alias}} = |f_{\text{signal}} - k \cdot f_s|$$

> [!WARNING]
> **The Phase Trap at the Boundary:** Even at exactly $f_s = 2 f_{\max}$, sampling can completely fail. If a 10 Hz sine wave is sampled at exactly 20 Hz and the sample instances coincide precisely with the zero-crossings ($t = 0, 0.05, 0.10, \dots$), every sample records exactly $0\mu\text{V}$. The entire oscillation vanishes. In practical neural engineering, systems oversample by $3\times\text{ to }10\times$ and utilize analog anti-aliasing low-pass filters prior to digitization.

### Downsampling Requires Anti-Aliasing
When downsampling a recorded signal offline (e.g., from 1000 Hz to 250 Hz), the new Nyquist limit drops from 500 Hz to 125 Hz. Any spectral energy between 125 Hz and 500 Hz must be removed with a digital anti-aliasing low-pass filter **before** decimating the array. Decimating without filtering folds out-of-band energy into the neural passband, corrupting it irreversibly.

## 3. Python Laboratory 1: Visualizing Aliasing

### Code Implementation (`test_aliasing.py`)

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# 1. Continuous biological ground truth: 10 Hz Alpha wave
f_signal = 10  # 10 Hz
duration = 1.0  # 1 second
t_continuous = np.linspace(0, duration, 1000)
signal_continuous = np.sin(2 * np.pi * f_signal * t_continuous)

# 2. Adequate sampling: 50 Hz (Safely above Nyquist limit of 20 Hz)
fs_good = 50
t_good = np.linspace(0, duration, int(fs_good * duration), endpoint=False)
signal_good = np.sin(2 * np.pi * f_signal * t_good)

# 3. Inadequate sampling: 12 Hz (Violates Nyquist limit: 12 Hz < 20 Hz)
fs_bad = 12
t_bad = np.linspace(0, duration, int(fs_bad * duration), endpoint=False)
signal_bad = np.sin(2 * np.pi * f_signal * t_bad)

# Diagnostics
print(f"Adequate sampling (50 Hz): {len(t_good)} samples | {fs_good/f_signal:.1f} samples/cycle")
print(f"Inadequate sampling (12 Hz): {len(t_bad)} samples | {fs_bad/f_signal:.1f} samples/cycle")
print(f"Predicted alias frequency: |10 - 12| = {abs(f_signal - fs_bad)} Hz")
```

### Quantitative Output & Interpretation

```text
Adequate sampling (50 Hz): 50 samples | 5.0 samples/cycle
Inadequate sampling (12 Hz): 12 samples | 1.2 samples/cycle
Predicted alias frequency: |10 - 12| = 2 Hz
```

- **50 Hz Sampling:** Provides 5 discrete points per 10 Hz cycle. Connecting the samples recovers the true 10 Hz periodicity and sinusoidal profile.
- **12 Hz Sampling:** Provides only 1.2 points per cycle. The discrete samples coincide with a phase-inverted 2 Hz wave ($|10 - 12| = 2\text{ Hz}$). 
- **AI Consequence:** A machine learning model trained on the 12 Hz sampled array will interpret this as a biological Delta oscillation (2 Hz) rather than an Alpha oscillation (10 Hz). No model architecture can resolve this ambiguity after sampling has occurred.

## 4. Topic 2: Contamination — Noise vs. Artifacts

Scalp EEG electrodes measure electrical potential differences across the scalp surface. The recorded voltage is an aggregate sum of neural fields and substantial non-neural sources:
- **Noise:** Uncorrelated, broadband, stochastic fluctuations (e.g., thermal resistance in electrode leads, internal amplifier noise).
- **Artifacts:** Structured, high-amplitude, non-neural voltages generated by cranial physiology, bodily movement, or environmental electromagnetic fields.

### The Four Primary EEG Artifacts

| Artifact Type | Physical / Physiological Mechanism | Typical Trace Signature | Primary Topography | AI Pitfall / Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Eye Blink / Ocular (EOG)** | Corneo-retinal electrostatic dipole ($+10\text{ to }+30\text{ mV}$ cornea relative to retina). Eyelid movement alters electric field projection. | High-amplitude ($50\text{–}200\mu\text{V}$), low-frequency ($1\text{–}3\text{ Hz}$) positive voltage monophasic surge. | Frontopolar electrodes ($F_{p1}, F_{p2}, F_z$). | Spectral overlap with neural Delta waves; AI models falsely classify blinks as deep sleep or cognitive slowing. |
| **Muscle Activity (EMG)** | Action potentials generated by motor units in cranial, facial, jaw, and neck musculature. | High-frequency ($20\text{–}200\text{ Hz}$), jagged, broadband bursts; amplitude $10\text{–}100\mu\text{V}$. | Temporal and peripheral channels ($T_7, T_8, O_1, O_2$). | Overlaps Beta ($13\text{–}30\text{ Hz}$) and Gamma ($>30\text{ Hz}$). Models memorize jaw tension as "cognitive stress". |
| **Electrode Movement / Pop** | Abrupt mechanical displacement of electrode or cable altering skin-electrolyte contact impedance. | Instantaneous step-jump ($20\text{–}100+\mu\text{V}$) or exponential baseline recovery. | Localized to single disturbed channel. | Massive localized variance skewing normalization and spatial covariance matrices. |
| **Power-Line Interference** | Electromagnetic induction from 50/60 Hz alternating current mains wiring coupling into electrode leads. | Relentless, perfectly periodic sinusoidal hum at exactly 50 Hz (EU/Asia) or 60 Hz (US). | Present across all channels with unshielded leads. | Model learns laboratory electrical environment; fails completely under hospital/regional domain shift. |

## 5. Python Laboratory 2: Simulating Realistic Multi-Component Contamination

### Code Implementation (`simulate_artifacts.py`)

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# 1. Underlying neural ground truth: 4 seconds at 250 Hz
np.random.seed(42)
fs = 250
duration = 4.0
t = np.linspace(0, duration, int(fs * duration), endpoint=False)  # 1000 samples
clean_eeg = 1.0 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 10 * t)  # 1 uV peak Alpha oscillation

# 2. Eye Blink: Gaussian-shaped surge centered at t = 1.5 s
blink_artifact = np.zeros_like(t)
blink_artifact += 25.0 * np.exp(-((t - 1.5)**2) / 0.02)  # 25 uV peak (25x neural signal)

# 3. Jaw-Muscle EMG Burst: High-frequency random burst between 2.5 s and 3.0 s
muscle_artifact = np.zeros_like(t)
jaw_clench_indices = (t >= 2.5) & (t <= 3.0)
muscle_artifact[jaw_clench_indices] = np.random.normal(0, 8.0, np.sum(jaw_clench_indices))

# 4. Electrode Movement: Baseline step of +20 uV occurring at t = 3.5 s
movement_artifact = np.zeros_like(t)
movement_artifact[t >= 3.5] = 20.0

# 5. Power-Line Interference: 50 Hz sinusoidal hum (5 uV peak)
line_noise = 5.0 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 50 * t)

# Composite observed EEG signal
observed_eeg = clean_eeg + blink_artifact + muscle_artifact + movement_artifact + line_noise

# Diagnostics
print(f"Clean EEG peak: {np.max(np.abs(clean_eeg)):.2f} uV")
print(f"Blink artifact peak: {np.max(blink_artifact):.2f} uV (Ratio: {np.max(blink_artifact)/np.max(np.abs(clean_eeg)):.1f}x)")
print(f"Movement step offset: {movement_artifact[-1]:.2f} uV")
print(f"Observed EEG variance: {np.var(observed_eeg):.2f} uV^2 vs Clean EEG variance: {np.var(clean_eeg):.2f} uV^2")
```

### Quantitative Output & Interpretation

```text
Clean EEG peak: 1.00 uV
Blink artifact peak: 25.00 uV (Ratio: 25.0x)
Movement step offset: 20.00 uV
Observed EEG variance: 73.18 uV^2 vs Clean EEG variance: 0.50 uV^2
```

- In this simulation, the clean neural Alpha wave possesses a variance of only $0.50\mu\text{V}^2$ ($A=1\mu\text{V}$).
- Contamination drives the observed variance to $73.18\mu\text{V}^2$—a $146\times$ increase.
- An uncalibrated AI model receiving this vector will optimize loss against the massive ocular pulse ($t=1.5\text{ s}$), muscle burst ($t=2.5\text{–}3.0\text{ s}$), or power-line carrier rather than the underlying neural state.

## 6. Day 2 Knowledge Checkpoint & Worked Solutions

### Question 1: Nyquist Math for Gamma Oscillations
> *You want to build a machine learning model that analyzes Gamma band oscillations specifically between 40 Hz and 70 Hz. What is the absolute mathematical minimum sampling rate you need to capture this information without aliasing? What would be a safer, practical sampling rate to choose?*

**Authoritative Solution:**
1. **Mathematical Minimum (Nyquist Rate):**
   The highest frequency component of interest is $f_{\max} = 70\text{ Hz}$.
   $$f_s > 2 f_{\max} = 2 \times 70\text{ Hz} = 140\text{ Hz}$$
   The theoretical lower bound is strictly $140\text{ Hz}$.
2. **Practical Engineering Selection:**
   A practical system requires an oversampling margin ($3\times\text{ to }5\times f_{\max}$) to accommodate the transition band of hardware anti-aliasing analog filters:
   - **$250\text{ Hz}$:** Standard, computationally lightweight, places the Nyquist limit at $125\text{ Hz}$, leaving a comfortable $55\text{ Hz}$ guard band above $70\text{ Hz}$.
   - **$500\text{ Hz}$:** Research standard for Gamma recordings, ensuring robust waveform morphology preservation and negligible phase distortion near $70\text{ Hz}$.

### Question 2: Artifact Diagnosis — Baseline Jump
> *You are reviewing a 10-second EEG trace. For the first 5 seconds, the amplitude fluctuates between $-10\mu\text{V}$ and $+10\mu\text{V}$. Suddenly, the entire waveform jumps up to $+50\mu\text{V}$ and stays there, oscillating around this new elevated baseline. Which of the four artifacts is most likely responsible, and why?*

**Authoritative Solution:**
- **Responsible Artifact:** **Electrode Movement / Electrode Contact Pop (Impedance Shift).**
- **Physical Reasoning:** Biological neural assemblies cannot physically maintain a sustained DC offset shift of $+50\mu\text{V}$ across an entire electrode lead. Eye blinks produce transient pulses returning to baseline within $200\text{–}400\text{ ms}$; muscle activity produces zero-mean high-frequency oscillatory bursts; power-line hum produces a symmetric zero-centered sine. An instantaneous step change that maintains a new DC baseline indicates an abrupt alteration in the electrochemical half-cell potential at the electrode–electrolyte–skin interface, typically caused by physical tugging of the electrode lead or head movement.

### Question 3: Biological Reasoning — 99% Accurate "Stress" Classifier
> *An untrained AI researcher trains a model to classify "Stressed" vs. "Relaxed" states. The model achieves 99% accuracy. Upon inspection, the model relies heavily on highly irregular, rapid, high-amplitude spikes located mostly in temporal and neck electrodes. Is the model actually reading "stressed" neural activity? What biological artifact has the model likely memorized instead?*

**Authoritative Solution:**
- **Scientific Verdict:** **No.** The model is not decoding neural stress; it has memorized an electromyographic (EMG) muscle artifact shortcut.
- **Physical Reasoning:** Temporal ($T_7, T_8$) and occipital/mastoid channels sit directly over cranial muscles (temporalis, frontalis, masseter, and cervical neck muscles). Psychological stress induces somatic motor tension—subconscious jaw clenching, teeth grinding, and neck rigidity. Action potentials from these somatic muscles produce high-amplitude ($20\text{–}100\mu\text{V}$), broadband ($20\text{–}200\text{ Hz}$) irregular bursts.
- **The Shortcut Learning Problem:** The classifier discovered an easy physical feature that correlates with the experimental label (`STRESSED`), but it has failed to learn central nervous system dynamics. If deployed on a paralyzed or deeply relaxed individual experiencing cognitive stress without overt muscle tension, the model will fail.

### Question 4: Environmental Context — Regional Domain Shift
> *An AI model trained entirely on clinical EEG data from hospitals in New Delhi is deployed in a hospital in New York. Its accuracy instantly drops. Based strictly on the physical artifacts discussed today, what environmental difference in the raw data might be destroying the model's predictions?*

**Authoritative Solution:**
- **Environmental Culprit:** **Alternating Current Power-Line Mains Frequency Discrepancy (50 Hz vs. 60 Hz).**
- **Physical Reason:** 
  - India (New Delhi), Europe, and the UK operate electrical grids at **$50\text{ Hz}$**.
  - The United States (New York) operates electrical grids at **$60\text{ Hz}$**.
- **Impact on AI Models:** If raw data was fed to the model without notch filtering, the neural network's convolutional kernels or spectral weights likely incorporated 50 Hz power-line features as an implicit baseline or normalization reference. When deployed in New York, the input contains a massive sinusoidal carrier at 60 Hz and zero energy at 50 Hz. This severe covariate shift / out-of-distribution artifact invalidates the model's learned weights.
