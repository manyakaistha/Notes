## 2. Seven Major AI Applications in Neuroscience

Artificial intelligence and deep learning architectures have revolutionized seven primary computational domains across clinical and cognitive neuroscience:

![Neuroscience Applications Landscape and Architecture Trade-offs](../assets/fig12_neuroscience_applications_landscape.png)

**Figure 1.** Each application begins with a measured input and an independently defined target. Different modalities require different tensor geometries and evaluation units; sharing a neural-network label does not make their scientific problems interchangeable.

### 2.1 Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCI)
- **Problem Formulation:** Mapping real-time user intent into control signals for external assistive devices (robotic prosthetics, neural communication spellers, exoskeletons) without peripheral neuromuscular involvement.
- **Input Geometry:** High-density multichannel EEG or invasive electrocorticography (ECoG), typically $[C \times T]$ tensors sampled at 128–1000 Hz.
- **Predominant Deep Architectures:** Compact 1D/2D CNNs (e.g., EEGNet, ShallowNet), spatial-temporal separable convolutions, and Riemannian geometric decoders.
- **Primary Failure Mode:** **Sensor non-stationarity and electrode impedance drift**. Changes in skin-electrode conductance, user cognitive fatigue, and sweat artifacts cause signal distributions to drift continuously over hours, requiring frequent recalibration.

### 2.2 Motor Imagery Decoding
- **Problem Formulation:** Classifying mental rehearsals of specific limb movements (e.g., left hand, right hand, foot, tongue) by detecting event-related desynchronization (ERD) and synchronization (ERS) over the primary motor cortex.
- **Input Geometry:** Sensorimotor montage (electrodes $C_3, C_z, C_4$) spanning 2.0–4.0 second epochs ($[3 \times 480]$ at 160 Hz).
- **Predominant Deep Architectures:** Spatiotemporal CNNs with temporal filter banks matching physiological $\mu$ (8–12 Hz) and $\beta$ (13–30 Hz) bands.
- **Primary Failure Mode:** **"BCI Illiteracy" and severe inter-subject variability**. Approximately 15–30% of human users do not produce detectable sensorimotor mu-rhythms on the scalp surface due to cortical anatomical differences or skull thickness.

### 2.3 Automated Seizure Detection and Prediction
- **Problem Formulation:** Automatically flagging epileptiform electrographic discharges or predicting impending ictal onset in continuous long-term clinical monitoring (ICU, epilepsy monitoring units).
- **Input Geometry:** Continuous 16–64 channel scalp EEG or intracranial depth/grid electrodes (iEEG) monitored over hours to days.
- **Predominant Deep Architectures:** Hybrid CNN-LSTM networks, Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs), and dilated causal convolutions.
- **Primary Failure Mode:** **Extreme class imbalance and high false-positive rates on physiological artifacts**. Pre-ictal and ictal states constitute less than 0.1% of continuous recordings; chewing, swallowing, or electrode popping artifacts frequently trigger false alarms that exhaust clinical staff.

### 2.4 Sleep Stage Classification
- **Problem Formulation:** Automating the clinical scoring of 30-second polysomnography (PSG) epochs into standardized AASM sleep stages: Wake, N1 (light sleep), N2 (sleep spindles/K-complexes), N3 (slow-wave sleep), and REM.
- **Input Geometry:** Multimodal arrays: EEG (central and occipital), electrooculogram (EOG for rapid eye movements), and electromyogram (EMG for muscle atonia).
- **Predominant Deep Architectures:** Dual-stream CNNs combined with bidirectional LSTMs or temporal Transformers modeling macro-sleep architecture across full 8-hour nights.
- **Primary Failure Mode:** **Subjective clinical inter-rater variability**. Certified human sleep technologists agree on sleep staging with only 80–85% concordance, particularly at ambiguous transitional boundaries (Wake $\to$ N1).

### 2.5 Affective Computing and Emotion Recognition
- **Problem Formulation:** Inferring affective states (valence, arousal, stress, cognitive workload) from neurophysiological correlates, such as frontal alpha asymmetry.
- **Input Geometry:** Frontal-prefrontal multichannel EEG montages ($F_p1, F_p2, F_3, F_4$) often augmented with galvanic skin response (GSR) and heart rate variability (HRV).
- **Predominant Deep Architectures:** Spatial-temporal graph neural networks (GNNs) and multi-branch CNNs.
- **Primary Failure Mode:** **Lack of objective ground truth and high subjectivity**. Human emotional states rely on retrospective self-reports (e.g., SAM scales) which are heavily confounded by social desirability, personality traits, and situational bias.

### 2.6 Neural Decoding of Motor Kinematics
- **Problem Formulation:** Reconstructing continuous limb kinematics (hand velocity, trajectory, joint angles) from invasive motor cortical ensemble activity to drive multi-axis robotic effectors.
- **Input Geometry:** High-density intracortical microelectrode arrays (e.g., Utah array) recording multi-unit spike trains and local field potentials (LFP) across 96–1000 channels.
- **Predominant Deep Architectures:** Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), LSTMs, and neural population dynamical models (e.g., LFADS).
- **Primary Failure Mode:** **Chronic microelectrode recording instability and neuron drop-out**. Biological glial scarring, micromotion, and foreign body tissue responses cause recorded single units to appear, vanish, or decouple from kinematics over weeks.

### 2.7 Neuroimaging Analysis and Disease Diagnosis
- **Problem Formulation:** Detecting neurodegenerative diseases (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's) or segmenting structural brain lesions from structural/functional magnetic resonance imaging (sMRI, fMRI) and diffusion tensor imaging (DTI).
- **Input Geometry:** Volumetric 3D spatial grids $[X \times Y \times Z]$ or 4D spatiotemporal tensors $[X \times Y \times Z \times T]$.
- **Predominant Deep Architectures:** 3D U-Nets, 3D CNNs, and Vision Transformers (ViTs).
- **Primary Failure Mode:** **The "curse of dimensionality" under tiny clinical cohort sizes**. A single 3D MRI volume contains millions of voxels, while clinical cohorts often contain fewer than 50–500 patients, leading to massive over-parameterization and scanner-site batch effects.

## 3. Cohort-Wide Empirical Benchmark: Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO)

To rigorously test whether deep representation learning overcomes biological variability, we execute a complete **10-fold Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO)** cross-validation benchmark across all 10 subjects ($S001$ to $S010$, Runs 4, 8, 12, $N = 450$ trials total).

### 3.1 Strict Leak-Free Protocol
In each fold $k \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$:
- **Training Partition:** 9 subjects ($N = 405$ trials). Channel-wise normalization (mean and standard deviation) is computed strictly on this partition.
- **Held-Out Test Partition:** 1 unseen subject ($N = 450 - 405 = 45$ trials). Evaluated in a zero-shot cross-subject manner.

We benchmark three distinct modeling paradigms:
1. **Dummy Baseline:** Predicts the most frequent class in the training fold.
2. **Logistic Regression (Classical):** $L_2$-regularized linear model trained on 15 handcrafted time- and frequency-domain features (Weeks 1–3).
3. **Compact 1D CNN (Deep):** `CompactEEGNet1D` (2,922 parameters) trained end-to-end on continuous multichannel voltage epochs $[B, 3, 480]$ without manual feature extraction.

## 4. Honest Scientific Analysis: The Biophysical Reality of Zero-Shot Decoding

The benchmark supports three descriptive findings:
1. **Modest Pooled Gain:** The CNN scores 55.3% accuracy and macro-F1 versus 52.7% accuracy and 52.6% macro-F1 for logistic regression. This is 12 additional correct predictions in 450 trials, not a demonstrated universal architecture advantage.
2. **Participant Variation:** Figure 3 shows CNN accuracies from 44.4% to 73.3%. Relative to logistic regression, it wins on 4 participants, ties on 1, and loses on 5. Each participant contributes 45 trials, so one prediction changes accuracy by 2.22 percentage points.
3. **Unidentified Causes:** These predictions do not measure anatomy, dipole orientation, imagery compliance, or source identity. Signal quality, subject shift, artifacts, and model instability remain plausible explanations requiring separate tests.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Compare published results only after checking task, subject selection, split unit, preprocessing, and test independence. High accuracy alone neither establishes leakage nor proves deployment reliability. [[week-4-report|Week 4 Report]] §6 provides the full metric audit and limitations.

## 6. Daily Knowledge Checkpoint

### Question 1: Mathematical Analysis of LOSO Variance
**Prompt:** In the 10-fold LOSO benchmark, the Compact 1D CNN had a mean subject accuracy of $55.3\%$ with standard deviation $\sigma = 10.0\%$, while Logistic Regression had a mean of $52.7\%$ with $\sigma = 5.1\%$.
1. Compute the 95% confidence interval of the mean accuracy for both models ($N=10$, using $t_{0.025, 9} \approx 2.262$).
2. Explain why the CNN exhibits nearly double the inter-subject variance of Logistic Regression.

**Solution:**
1. The standard error of the mean (SEM) is $\text{SEM} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{10}} \approx \frac{\sigma}{3.162}$.
   - **Logistic Regression:**
     $$\text{SEM}_{LR} = \frac{5.1\%}{3.162} = 1.61\%$$
     $$\text{CI}_{95\%} = 52.7\% \pm (2.262 \times 1.61\%) = 52.7\% \pm 3.64\% = [49.06\%, 56.34\%]$$
   - **Compact 1D CNN:**
     $$\text{SEM}_{CNN} = \frac{10.0\%}{3.162} = 3.16\%$$
     $$\text{CI}_{95\%} = 55.3\% \pm (2.262 \times 3.16\%) = 55.3\% \pm 7.15\% = [48.15\%, 62.45\%]$$
2. Logistic Regression operates on broad, fixed frequency bands (e.g., total power in 8–12 Hz and 13–30 Hz) which smooth out individual spectral variations, leading to consistent but mediocre performance across all subjects. In contrast, the 1D CNN learns high-capacity, sharp temporal filters tuned to the specific oscillatory waveforms dominant in the 9 training subjects. When a test subject shares these exact waveform shapes ($S04, S08$), the CNN decodes them with high accuracy (68–73%). However, when a test subject has a disparate peak frequency or inverted scalp dipole orientation ($S05, S06, S09$), the sharp filter fails completely, causing large performance swings.

### Question 2: BCI Illiteracy and Cortical Anatomy
**Prompt:** Explain why a subject might be unable to control a sensorimotor-rhythm BCI despite exerting maximal mental effort during motor imagery tasks. Relate your explanation to pyramidal dipole orientation and volume conduction.

**Solution:**
Scalp EEG electrodes measure extracellular potentials generated by open-field dipolar configurations of pyramidal neurons aligned perpendicular to the cortical surface. In the primary motor cortex (Brodmann area 4), the hand representation lies largely within the anterior bank of the central sulcus. 
Depending on individual cortical folding morphology:
1. If the sulcal wall runs parallel to the skull surface, the dipole vector points radially toward electrode $C_3$, generating a strong, easily detectable surface voltage.
2. If an individual's motor cortex is folded deeply or oriented tangentially/oppositely, the positive and negative poles of adjacent gyral banks cancel out before reaching the skull surface (closed-field geometry).
The individual is executing genuine motor imagery and generating robust intracortical activity, but the resulting electrical currents cannot penetrate through the meninges, skull, and scalp to produce a surface signal.

### Question 3: Inductive Bias Hierarchy
**Prompt:** Rank the following four architectures in order of increasing inductive bias for multichannel time-series data: Fully Connected MLP, 1D CNN, Vanilla RNN, Transformer. Justify your ordering.

**Solution:**
From least inductive bias (most flexible) to most inductive bias (most structurally constrained):
1. **Fully Connected MLP (Least Bias):** Treats all inputs as an unstructured bag of features. No assumptions about spatial proximity, temporal ordering, or shift invariance.
2. **Transformer:** Assumes tokens can interact with arbitrary non-local partners via pairwise attention. Does not assume local continuity or sequential progression; temporal order must be artificially injected via positional encodings.
3. **Vanilla RNN:** Imposes a strict causal sequential inductive bias: information must flow step-by-step through a continuous Markovian chain, assuming past states influence future states through recursive accumulation.
4. **1D CNN (Most Bias for Local Waveforms):** Imposes both strict **temporal locality** (units in early layers can only interact with immediate temporal neighbors within the kernel window) and **temporal translation invariance** (the identical filter operates across all points in time). This rigid constraint precisely matches the physical structure of localized oscillatory brain waves.
