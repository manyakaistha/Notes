## 1. Day 4 Overview & Daily Goals

When physical fields vary across multiple spatial dimensions as well as time (e.g., fluid velocity fields, acoustic pressure waves, temperature distributions), conservation laws take the form of **Partial Differential Equations (PDEs)**.

The primary goals of Day 4 are:
1. Classify linear second-order partial differential operators into **Elliptic**, **Parabolic**, and **Hyperbolic** regimes based on discriminant analysis.
2. Formalize the three canonical physical archetypes: Laplace/Poisson (equilibrium), Heat/Diffusion (dissipation), and Wave (reversible transport).
3. Rigorously define boundary conditions: Dirichlet (prescribed state), Neumann (prescribed flux), and Robin (mixed).
4. Implement the analytical solution to the 1D Heat Equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

## 2. Diagnostic & Previous-Day Verification

### Diagnostic Questions & Authoritative Solutions

#### 1. Why does an ODE require $n$ initial conditions, whereas a PDE requires boundary conditions across the entire boundary $\partial\Omega$?
- An ODE's domain is a 1D interval $[0, T]$ whose boundary consists of discrete isolated endpoints ($t=0$). A PDE domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ possesses a continuous bounding hypersurface $\partial\Omega$. Uniqueness of solutions to partial differential operators requires constraining the state or flux along this entire continuous perimeter.

#### 2. What physical phenomenon does the Laplacian operator $\nabla^2 u = \Delta u = \sum_{i=1}^d \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x_i^2}$ measure?
- The Laplacian measures the difference between the local value of a field $u(\mathbf{x})$ and the average value of the field in an infinitesimal sphere surrounding $\mathbf{x}$. If $\nabla^2 u > 0$, the local value is lower than the local neighborhood average; if $\nabla^2 u < 0$, it is a local peak. Thus, $\alpha \nabla^2 u$ drives smoothing and diffusion toward equilibrium.

#### 3. What is the physical distinction between an initial condition and a boundary condition?
- **Initial Condition:** Prescribes system state across the entire spatial domain $\Omega$ at the single initial timestamp $t=0$.
- **Boundary Condition:** Prescribes system state or normal derivative along the spatial perimeter $\partial\Omega$ for all timestamps $t \in [0, T]$.

## 3. Core Theory: Classification of Second-Order PDEs

### 3.1 The Discriminant Criterion
Consider the general linear second-order PDE in coordinates $(x, y)$:

$$A \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + B \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x \partial y} + C \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} + D \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + E \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + F u = G$$

By analogy to conic sections, the discriminant $\Delta = B^2 - 4AC$ dictates the physical nature of information propagation:

1. **Elliptic ($\Delta < 0$):** No real characteristic curves. Information travels infinitely fast in all directions. Governs static equilibrium and steady-state fields. Prototype: Laplace Equation $\nabla^2 u = 0$.
2. **Parabolic ($\Delta = 0$):** One set of real characteristic curves parallel to the time axis. Governs irreversible diffusion and entropy generation. Prototype: Heat Equation $\partial_t u = \alpha \nabla^2 u$.
3. **Hyperbolic ($\Delta > 0$):** Two sets of intersecting real characteristics. Information propagates at finite wave speeds along characteristics without dissipation. Prototype: Wave Equation $\partial_{tt} u = c^2 \nabla^2 u$.

### 3.2 The 1D Space-Time Heat Equation
Consider heat conduction along a 1D bar of length $L=1$ with thermal diffusivity $\alpha = 0.5$:

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}, \quad x \in [0, 1], \quad t \in [0, T]$$

- **Initial Condition:** $u(0, x) = \sin(\pi x)$
- **Dirichlet Boundary Conditions:** $u(t, 0) = 0, \quad u(t, 1) = 0$

Using separation of variables $u(t, x) = T(t) X(x)$:
$$\frac{T'(t)}{\alpha T(t)} = \frac{X''(x)}{X(x)} = -\lambda$$
Boundary conditions require $X(x) = \sin(n \pi x)$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_n = (n\pi)^2$. For $n=1$, the exact physical solution is:

$$u(t, x) = e^{-\alpha \pi^2 t} \sin(\pi x)$$

## 4. Practical Laboratory: Analytical Evaluation of the 1D Heat Field

### 4.1 Concept → Code → Output → Interpretation

#### 1. Concept
We compute the exact analytical temperature profile $u(t, x)$ and verify that the spatial curvature $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}$ is exactly balanced by the temporal rate of cooling $\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}$ at the center of the bar $x=0.5$ at $t = 0.1\text{ s}$.

#### 2. Code
```python
import numpy as np

alpha = 0.5
t_eval = 0.1
x_eval = 0.5

# Analytical field: u(t, x) = exp(-alpha * pi^2 * t) * sin(pi * x)
decay_factor = np.exp(-alpha * (np.pi**2) * t_eval)
u_val = decay_factor * np.sin(np.pi * x_eval)

# Derivatives computed analytically
# du/dt = -alpha * pi^2 * exp(-alpha * pi^2 * t) * sin(pi * x)
du_dt = -alpha * (np.pi**2) * decay_factor * np.sin(np.pi * x_eval)

# du/dx = pi * exp(-alpha * pi^2 * t) * cos(pi * x)
# d2u/dx2 = -pi^2 * exp(-alpha * pi^2 * t) * sin(pi * x)
d2u_dx2 = -(np.pi**2) * decay_factor * np.sin(np.pi * x_eval)

# Physics Residual: R = du/dt - alpha * d2u/dx2
pde_residual = du_dt - alpha * d2u_dx2

print(f"Evaluation Point: t = {t_eval} s, x = {x_eval}")
print(f"Temperature u(t, x):       {u_val:.6f}")
print(f"Time Derivative du/dt:     {du_dt:.6f} K/s")
print(f"Spatial Curvature d2u/dx2: {d2u_dx2:.6f} K/m^2")
print(f"Scaled Diffusion alpha*u_xx: {alpha * d2u_dx2:.6f}")
print(f"Physics Residual R:        {pde_residual:.10e}")
```

#### 3. Output
```text
Evaluation Point: t = 0.1 s, x = 0.5
Temperature u(t, x):       0.610500
Time Derivative du/dt:     -3.012571 K/s
Spatial Curvature d2u/dx2: -6.025143 K/m^2
Scaled Diffusion alpha*u_xx: -3.012571
Physics Residual R:        0.0000000000e+00
```

#### 4. Interpretation
At the spatial midpoint $x=0.5$, the initial temperature $1.0$ has cooled to $0.610500$ by $t=0.1\text{ s}$. The instantaneous cooling rate is $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -3.012571\text{ K/s}$. The spatial second derivative is negative (concave down), evaluating to $-6.025143\text{ K/m}^2$. Multiplying by $\alpha = 0.5$ yields $-3.012571$, which cancels $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}$ identically. The calculated residual is exactly $0.000000$, validating that the continuous function satisfies the governing PDE to machine precision.

## 5. Daily Knowledge Mastery Checkpoint

### Problem 1: PDE Classification
**Question:** Classify the following partial differential equation into elliptic, parabolic, or hyperbolic:
$$3 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} - 4 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x \partial y} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} + 2 \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 0$$
**Authoritative Solution:**  
Identify coefficients of second-order terms:
$$A = 3, \quad B = -4, \quad C = 1$$
Compute the discriminant:
$$\Delta = B^2 - 4AC = (-4)^2 - 4(3)(1) = 16 - 12 = 4$$
Since $\Delta = 4 > 0$, the partial differential equation is strictly **Hyperbolic**.

### Problem 2: Physical Boundary Condition Interpretation
**Question:** An engineer specifies $\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}(t, 0) = 0$ at the left boundary of a thermal rod. What does this mean physically, and what class of boundary condition is it?  
**Authoritative Solution:**  
By Fourier's Law of Heat Conduction, heat flux is proportional to the negative spatial temperature gradient: $q = -k \frac{\partial T}{\partial x}$. Setting $\frac{\partial T}{\partial x} = 0$ means that the conductive heat flux through the boundary is zero, modeling a **perfectly thermally insulated (adiabatic) wall**. Because it constrains the spatial directional derivative rather than the state value, it is a homogeneous **Neumann boundary condition**.
