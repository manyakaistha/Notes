# Autocorrelation and Pitch Estimation

The **Autocorrelation Sequence** $r_{xx}[l]$ measures the statistical self-similarity of a discrete-time signal $x[n]$ with a time-delayed copy of itself as a function of integer lag $l$:

$$r_{xx}[l] = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n] x[n-l]$$

### Fundamental Mathematical Properties
1. **Zero-Lag Energy Maximum:** At lag $l = 0$, $r_{xx}[0] = \sum x^2[n] = E_x$, the total signal energy. By Cauchy-Schwarz, $|r_{xx}[l]| \le r_{xx}[0]$ for all $l$.
2. **Even Symmetry:** For any real-valued sequence, $r_{xx}[l] = r_{xx}[-l]$.
3. **Periodicity Preservation:** If $x[n]$ is periodic with fundamental period $N_0$ (i.e., $x[n] = x[n + N_0]$), then $r_{xx}[l]$ is also periodic with the identical fundamental period $N_0$.

### Speech Application: Pitch Tracking
In human speech, voiced sounds (vowels, nasals, voiced fricatives) are generated by quasi-periodic vocal fold oscillations. While vocal tract filtering introduces complex local peaks in the time waveform, the autocorrelation sequence filters out phase variations and concentrates prominent local maxima at integer multiples of the pitch period:

$$l^* = \arg\max_{l \in [L_{\min}, L_{\max}]} r_{xx}[l] \implies F_0 = \frac{f_s}{l^*}$$

For adult males ($F_0 \approx 100\ \mathrm{Hz}$), $l^* \approx 160$ samples at $16\ \mathrm{kHz}$; for females ($F_0 \approx 250\ \mathrm{Hz}$), $l^* \approx 64$ samples. In unvoiced speech (e.g., /s/, /t/), $r_{xx}[l]$ decays rapidly to zero without secondary peaks.
