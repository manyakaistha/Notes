# The Speech Chain

The **Speech Chain** is the multidisciplinary communication pipeline describing the end-to-end transformation of an abstract thought in the speaker's brain into a decoded linguistic message in the listener's brain. First formalized by Denes and Pinson (1963), it categorizes speech transmission into five discrete operational stages:

1. **Linguistic Level (Speaker):** Neural selection and syntax arrangement of phonemes and lexical items.
2. **Physiological Level (Speaker):** Neuromuscular motor innervation of vocal tract articulators (diaphragm, vocal folds, velum, tongue, lips).
3. **Acoustic Level:** Physical propagation of continuous longitudinal pressure waves (compressions and rarefactions) through the gaseous transmission medium.
4. **Physiological Level (Listener):** Peripheral auditory transduction, where acoustic pressure waves vibrate the tympanic membrane and induce frequency-selective tonotopic oscillations along the basilar membrane in the cochlea.
5. **Linguistic Level (Listener):** Auditory nerve electro-chemical pulse transmission to the primary auditory cortex and subsequent phonetic decoding into lexical meaning.

### Why It Matters Here
Speech technology systems are engineered directly to emulate or interface with specific links in this chain. Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) mirrors stages 3 through 5, while Text-to-Speech (TTS) synthesizers execute stages 1 through 3.
