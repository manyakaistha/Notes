# Speech Perception and Perceptual Masking

**Formal definition.** Auditory masking is a change in the detectability of one sound (the maskee) caused by another sound (the masker). In frequency masking, energy in one auditory frequency region raises the detection threshold for nearby components, with the amount and spread depending on masker level, frequency, bandwidth, and listening conditions. Temporal masking occurs when a strong event affects perception of a weaker event shortly before or after it. Masking is therefore a property of auditory perception under specified conditions; it is not equivalent to deleting spectral bins or applying a universal fixed threshold. Psychoacoustic models approximate these effects with frequency-dependent critical-band or auditory-filter representations and level-dependent thresholds.

**Why it matters here.** Week 2 connects speech perception and frequency analysis to perceptual masking, cepstral filtering, and feature extraction. Masking motivates frequency-selective representations while reminding us that a measured spectrum and perceived prominence are distinct quantities.
